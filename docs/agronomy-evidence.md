# Peshawar Valley Agronomy Evidence & Decision Support Model

## 1. Regional Context: Peshawar Valley, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa
Peshawar Valley (covering Peshawar, Charsadda, Nowshera, and Mardan districts) is a fertile agricultural basin in northwestern Pakistan flanked by the Kabul and Swat rivers. The farming systems are dominated by intensive double-cropping:

- **Rabi (Winter Season):** November to April (Wheat, Berseem clover, Mustard, Lentil, Chickpea).
- **Kharif (Summer Monsoon Season):** June to October (Maize, Rice, Sugarcane, Mung bean).
- **Zaid (Spring/Transition Window):** April to June (Short-duration pulses, forage).

## 2. Key Climate & Environmental Stressors
1. **Terminal Heat Stress on Wheat:** In late March and April, temperatures frequently spike above 32°C during grain fill, accelerating maturity and causing shriveled grains. NASA POWER `T2M_MAX` tracks this vulnerability.
2. **Peak Summer Evapotranspiration:** In May and June, temperatures exceed 40°C with high solar radiation (`ALLSKY_SFC_SW_DWN`). Heavy water crops like summer maize or sugarcane severely strain groundwater and canal rotations.
3. **Monsoon Timing & Humidity:** Monsoon rains arrive in July-August (`PRECTOTCORR`). High relative humidity (`RH2M`) elevates risk of fungal pathogens (e.g. Ascochyta blight in chickpea, foliar blights in maize).

## 3. Water Regimes & Irrigation Realities
- **Canal Warabandi:** Rotational water rights distributed weekly. Water turns are fixed regardless of immediate crop evapotranspiration needs.
- **Tube-Wells:** Used for supplemental irrigation. High operational cost due to electricity load shedding and diesel fuel expenses.
- **Water Efficiency Imperative:** Introducing low-water crops (pulses, oilseeds) during high-demand months directly mitigates financial and operational stress on smallholders.

## 4. Cropping Scenarios Evaluated
1. **Baseline (Conventional): Wheat – Maize**
   - High cereal yield, familiar marketing channels, high reliance on synthetic urea and diammonium phosphate (DAP). High summer water pumping costs.
2. **Pulse-Inclusive: Wheat – Mung Bean (or Chickpea – Maize)**
   - Biological nitrogen fixation via symbiotic *Rhizobium* bacteria reduces chemical fertilizer needs for succeeding cereal crops. Short maturity lowers water demand during peak heat windows.
3. **Soil-Cover / Forage: Berseem Clover – Maize**
   - Multi-cut winter legume providing continuous green canopy cover, suppressing winter weeds, maximizing organic matter return, and supplying high-demand urban dairy markets.

## 5. Evidence Standards & Scientific Limits
- **Documented Evidence:** Agronomic practices and crop windows supported by peer-reviewed literature, KP Agricultural Research Institute (Tarnab, Peshawar), and FAO guidelines.
- **Tentative Guidance:** Climate-rotation adaptation scores and suitability indices that require local validation with extension workers.
- **No Absolute Guarantees:** AgroShift does not provide guaranteed yield forecasts, profit promises, or exact irrigation prescriptions. All recommendations are presented as probabilistic decision support.
