The Local Challenge Peshawar Valley Agronomic Realities

Farmers need clear answers, not complicated satellite data.

Changing weather makes crop planning harder every season.

Peshawar Valley Farmers Face:

  • • Rising Heat Extremes: May–June heat waves scorch early summer seedlings.
  • • Monsoon Variability: Irregular precipitation patterns disrupt sowing calendars.
  • • Canal & Tube Well Deficits: Unpredictable rotation turns and power outages.
  • • Post-Harvest Bare Soil: Prolonged fallow periods desiccate fertile topsoil.
Critical Farmer Questions
"What should I grow next?"
"Will my field have enough water?"
"How can I protect my soil?"
Rising Heat Stress

Uncertain Rainfall Patterns
GPM IMERG multi-year analysis reveals intensifying dry spells and erratic monsoon arrival timing.
Root-Zone Moisture Depletion
SMAP Level 4 signals sub-surface moisture dropping below 30% saturation before sowing windows close.

NASA Earth Observations Multi-Sensor Science Stack

NASA satellites help us see what the field cannot always show.

AgroShift combines four NASA Earth-observation constellations into one transparent checkup.

NASA Source What It Helps Us Understand How AgroShift Uses It
NASA POWER
Daily Point Agroclimatology API
 Temperature (T2M), precipitation, solar radiation, humidity, and 10-year climatological baseline. Identifies heat-stress windows, frost probabilities, and baseline weather anomalies. Primary climate engine.
NASA SMAP / Crop-CASMA
L4 Global Surface & Root-Zone Soil Moisture
 0–5 cm surface wetness and 0–100 cm root-zone available water profile. Flags root-zone dry spells, water-logging risks, and verifies sub-surface moisture trends before sowing.
NASA GPM IMERG
Integrated Multi-satellitE Retrievals for GPM
 High-resolution satellite precipitation estimates and cumulative rainfall deficit. Calculates 30-day precipitation deficit index and consecutive dry-spell runs across irrigation zones.
NASA MODIS / VIIRS NDVI
Normalized Difference Vegetation Index
 Vegetation canopy greenness, photosynthetic activity, and biomass vigor. Detects low-greenness canopy stress and verifies bare-field exposure periods across crop transitions.
NASA Satellites (POWER, SMAP, GPM, MODIS)
➔
AgroShift Science Engine (Normalization & Null Integrity)
➔
Farmer Decision Plans (3 Transparent Rotations)
🛰️ Core Scientific Rule: NASA values `<= -900` are strictly mapped to `null` (not zeros) — preventing false zeroes from distorting farm decisions.

Impact & Ethics Scientific Rigor & Responsible AI

Better decisions begin with clearer information.

AgroShift supports farmers without pretending to guarantee outcomes.

🌱

Farmer Impact

  • • Simple, transparent starting point for crop planning
  • • Side-by-side trade-off analysis of water vs. soil health
  • • Voice-assisted Urdu/English accessible inputs
  • • Portable PDF/CSV field plans for offline use
👥

Community Impact

  • • Calibrated for Peshawar, Charsadda, and Mardan districts
  • • Supports regional water-table preservation
  • • Empowers agricultural extension advisors in the field
  • • Encourages legume integration to cut synthetic fertilizer dependency
🛡️

Scientific Honesty

  • • Satellites observe broad regional pixels, not farm micro-truth
  • • Missing NASA data remains missing (no synthetic interpolation)
  • • Zero claims of guaranteed yields, profits, or water delivery
  • • Decadal trends require at least 5 continuous years
⚠️
Mandatory Local Check Disclaimer
